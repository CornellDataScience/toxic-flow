1. Executive Summary

The simulator answers one question for each hypothetical order: if we had rested a passive order at this price at this moment, how much of it would have filled, when, and how sure are we?

It replays recorded Kraken L3 messages through the existing L3Book while a separate per-order QueueTracker follows the hypothetical order’s place in the FIFO queue using order IDs and quantities. The historical book is never modified by hypothetical orders, so checksum validation and reconstruction stay exactly as they are today.

Three ideas carry the design:

  1. Hypothetical orders live beside the book, not in it. Each order owns a small shadow queue (the set of order IDs that were ahead of it at insertion). Historical events update that shadow queue; the L3Book is read-only to the simulator.

  2. Only a narrow class of events can fill us, and only those need execution attribution. Whether an order ahead of us traded or was canceled does not change our queue progress, because either way it leaves. Fills come only from reductions that are (a) at our price or behind it, (b) at the touch or part of a sweep through it, and (c) after everything ahead of us is gone. L3 gives the event kind but not the reason, so every candidate fill carries an evidence label, and one pass yields conservative / balanced / optimistic results as bounds.

  3. Time discipline prevents lookahead. Stream order, not timestamps, is the clock. A strategy sees only items whose exchange timestamp is strictly before its decision time. Data gaps and desyncs censor orders; they are never counted as “unfilled”.

Findings from reading the repository that shaped the design

  • Trade prints are recorded, but in a different stream (data/raw/public/, a separate websocket connection) than L3 (data/raw/l3/). Trades carry taker side, price, quantity and a trade_id, but no maker order ID [Kraken docs]. Attribution therefore has to be inferred by matching (§6.5).

  • L3Book.apply consumes a whole per-symbol message item atomically and offers no per-event hook, no pre-event state, and no ordered-level accessor [Code]. A thin subclass (SimL3Book) adds those without touching the production class (§5, §8).

  • The recorder’s metadata stores price_precision and qty_precision but not tick size [Code]. improve_quote and quote_deeper need a tick; the instrument message carries tick_size but it is not stored, so the simulator derives it or reads config (see §13.1 and OQ-6).

  • A snapshot in the middle of a stream is a discontinuity marker: the recorder resyncs after checksum failures, reconnects and hourly rotation, and events between the unsubscribe and the new snapshot are not in the file [Code] (§4.2, §7).

  • An L3 event’s own timestamp is documented as “the time the order was inserted or amended”, so add events produced when a deeper level re-enters the subscribed depth probably carry old timestamps [Kraken docs; Verify]. The item-level timestamp (matching-engine message time) is the usable clock.

In scope

  • Offline replay of recorded L3 data for one or more symbols.

  • Placement schedule → strategy decision → hypothetical order → queue tracking → labeled fill candidates → per-policy outcomes.

4.2 How a historical L3 event reaches the book today

  1. Collection. scripts/record.py calls fetch_pair_info for precisions, creates Recorder(root, 'l3', meta) and a BookStream on wss://ws-l3.kraken.com/v2 with one L3Book per symbol and on_raw=rec.write.

  2. Per message. BookStream._handle parses with Decimal, skips subscribe/unsubscribe acknowledgements, and calls on_raw before applying the message, so the raw text (including a message that later fails its checksum) is always archived verbatim with a local recv_ns.

  3. Applying. For each item in msg['data'] (one symbol each), L3Book.apply(msg['type'], item) runs. On a checksum failure the stream resets that book and unsubscribes/resubscribes, which produces a new snapshot later in the same file.

  4. Rotation. Each UTC hour maybe_rotate() opens a new file with a meta line and rotate_files calls resync(), but updates from the old subscription can arrive before the new snapshot: 12 of 13 real L3 files begin with an update, not a snapshot (see §4.6).

  5. Offline. read_recording yields records; validate_recording.main creates an L3Book per symbol, applies items in order and counts checksum_ok, checksum_fail and skipped_unsynced.

Consequence for replay. A recording is a sequence of synchronized segments. Segment boundaries are visible only indirectly: a meta line (new writer session), a snapshot after the first one, a local-receive-time gap, or a failed checksum. The simulator turns each of these into an explicit QualityEvent (§7).

What this means for the design

  1. Discontinuities are frequent, so censoring will matter. 406 snapshots against 26 expected from startup and hourly rotation (13 files x 2 symbols) means about 376 mid-stream resyncs from checksum failures. That is roughly 14 per symbol-hour, one every four minutes on average, if they are evenly spread (the audit did not report their distribution). Under that rough assumption a horizon of 10 s overlaps a discontinuity for about 4% of orders and 60 s for about a quarter, before the coverage filter in §11.4. Phase 0 should measure the real spacing and pick the horizon with it.

  2. Partial fills are rare in L3. Modify is about 0.3% of events (26k of about 9.7M), while deletes are 36%. Rank 3 evidence will seldom fire, most fills will appear as full deletes (rank 2), and the L3-only bounds will be wide. That is why Phase 3 is now required rather than optional (§6.5.3, §12).

  3. The streams are independent. Trades and L3 are separate websockets, recorders and files with no sequence numbers between them; each resyncs and reconnects on its own. Joining them by exchange timestamp (§6.5.5) is the only safe option, and TradeIndex must check coverage per interval.

  4. Do not assume files start synced. Treat every snapshot as a reset and drop updates until the first one (already the design; now confirmed by data).

  5. The gap and restart paths are untested on real data. There were no receive gaps and no same-hour restarts, so those code paths are covered by synthetic tests only (§10.2, scenarios 13 to 16).

  6. Depth. L3 depth counts price levels (100 here), so orders beyond 100 levels are not tracked; OUT_OF_DEPTH covers it.

5. Proposed Architecture (Req. B)

5.1 Component diagram

The engine reads recorded items in file order and advances the historical book only through SimL3Book. It builds one read-only BookView per decision, hands the same view to every strategy, and routes book events to one QueueTracker per live order. Fills are labeled candidates, not facts; results leave through ResultWriter.

5.3 What happens for each item

  1. The reader yields a record. When the local recv_ns jumps by more than gap_ns it first yields a gap marker, which the engine turns into a RECV_GAP discontinuity; an unreadable file tail yields a truncated marker.

  2. For each item: parse the exchange timestamp to integer nanoseconds. A snapshot after the first synced one is a RESNAPSHOT discontinuity and is handled before anything else.

  3. Fire every action with due_ts <= ts_ns (expiries, decisions, insertions). This is the strict-before rule.

  4. SimL3Book.apply() advances the book, puts the item’s BookEvents in last_events and returns the checksum result.

  5. If the result is False or None, discard the events and censor or skip. If True, classify evidence once per event, then let each live order’s tracker consume the events in order.

  6. Post-item checks: level still within subscribed depth, order complete, optional invariant audit.

  7. At end of data: censor live orders, record unreached requests, write results.

5.5 Dependency rules

  • Strategies depend only on BookView and PlacementRequest. They never see order size, trades, fills or the future.

  • QueueTracker depends only on BookEvent plus its own side, price and ahead set.

  • TradeIndex is never passed to a strategy; classification looks only inside a bounded window around the event’s own timestamp.

  • Only SimL3Book.apply() writes to the book. Everything else reads copies.

  • Only the engine imports every component; scripts and tests import the engine.

6. Queue-Aware Execution Simulation (Req. C)

6.1 Model and invariants

The model has three separate parts: the historical book (read-only truth), each hypothetical order (state held outside the book), and an evidence layer that says how believable each candidate fill is. Queue position comes from the book events; fills come from the subset of events that could have reached our order.

6.2 Inserting a hypothetical order

Insertion happens at a book boundary, never in the middle of an item.

  1. Decision boundary. A request with timestamp T fires before the first item whose timestamp is at least T (§7.1). The book then holds exactly the items with timestamp below T.

  2. One shared view. The engine builds a single BookView (top levels, best bid and ask, tick) and passes the same object to all three strategies, so every strategy has identical information.

  3. Strategy output. Each strategy returns an OrderIntent(side, price) or a Skip(reason). The engine attaches the fixed size from config.

  4. Insertion boundary. With latency_ns = 0 insertion happens at the same boundary. With latency L the intent is held and inserted before the first item with timestamp at least T + L; the price stays as decided, but the queue is read at insertion time.

  5. Passivity check. A buy must have price < best_ask; a sell must have price > best_bid. Otherwise the order ends as REJECTED_CROSSED_AT_INSERT. This can only trigger when latency is non-zero.

  6. Visibility check. The price must be inside the subscribed depth (level_visible), otherwise OUT_OF_DEPTH.

  7. Ahead snapshot. level_orders(side, price) returns a copy of the level as (order_id, qty) pairs in priority order. If the level does not exist (typical for improve_quote) the ahead set is empty. The tracker stores ahead_ids, ahead_qty, and the initial queue_ahead_qty, queue_ahead_orders, touch_qty and level_rank for the output schema.

  8. Registration. The order is added to the live set and an EXPIRE action is queued for inserted_ts + horizon.

Priority rules come from Kraken: orders at the same price fill in time order, a quantity reduction keeps priority, and a quantity increase or price change sends the order to the back of the queue [Kraken docs]. L3Book mirrors the first two through dict insertion order.

The reach test (rows 3 to 6) is the decisive function of the whole design:

def reach(order, ev):
    removed = ev.prior.qty - (ev.qty if ev.kind == 'modify' else 0)   # delete removes all
    if removed <= 0:                       return None   # qty increase or no-op
    p = ev.prior.price                                    # where the order sat BEFORE the event
    if ev.kind == 'modify' and ev.price != p:  return None   # re-queue, not a fill
    if better_than(order.side, p, order.price): return None   # priced ahead of us
    if p == order.price and order.ahead_ids:   # someone is still ahead of us
        order.diag['fifo_anomaly'] += 1;        return None
    if ev.best_before != p:                    return None   # executions happen only at the touch
    mech = 'level' if p == order.price else 'intercept'
    return ReachEvent(ev.ref, ev.item_ts_ns, p, removed, mech)

best_before is the best price on that side immediately before the event is applied (in code: BookEvent.best_bid_before for a bids event, best_ask_before for an asks event). SimL3Book records it per event, so a sweep that clears several levels inside one item is handled correctly: after the first level’s orders are deleted, best_before for later events has moved to the next level.

intercept covers the case where our price is better than the level that actually traded. A taker that historically hit a worse level would have hit our better-priced order first, so those reductions are ours. This is the main fill path for improve_quote.

6.4 Queue advancement versus executed volume

Our queue progress does not depend on why an order ahead of us left. An order ahead of us that traded and one that canceled both shorten the queue. Only reductions behind the ahead set (or at worse prices) can fill us, so attribution only has to be solved for reachable events (§6.5).

6.5.4 Trade matching (Phase 3)

Trades are loaded once into per-symbol arrays sorted by exchange timestamp. For each trusted item, group the structurally plausible reductions by (side, price) and match FIFO, because executions always consume the front of a queue first.

def classify_item(symbol, events, trades, tol):
    out = {}
    for (side, price), group in group_plausible_reductions(events):       # in event order = FIFO
        prints = trades.window(symbol, taker=opposite(side), price=price,
                               ts=group[0].event_ts_ns, before=tol.before, after=tol.after)
        pool = sum(t.qty for t in prints)
        healthy = trades.feed_healthy(symbol, group[0].item_ts_ns, tol.health_span)
        for ev in group:
            if pool >= ev.removed - EPS:
                exact = any(abs(t.ts_ns - ev.event_ts_ns) <= tol.exact for t in prints)
                out[ev.ref] = (TRADE_CONFIRMED if exact else TRADE_PLAUSIBLE, trade_ids(prints))
                pool -= ev.removed
            elif healthy:
                out[ev.ref] = (TRADE_ABSENT, ())
            else:                                                          # trade feed has a hole
                out[ev.ref] = (L3_MODIFY_DOWN if ev.kind == 'modify' else L3_DELETE_AMBIGUOUS, ())
    return out

A partially covered reduction counts only for the covered part. feed_healthy is true when trade_id is contiguous across the window and the trade stream has no receive gap. Tolerances (exact, before, after) come from the Phase 0 audit, not from guesswork.

6.5.5 Joining two streams

L3 and trades arrive on separate websocket connections hosted on different market-data stacks, so the join key is the exchange timestamp, never recv_ns or file order. TradeIndex is an in-memory sorted array with bisect lookups, so no streaming merge is needed. The lookup window is bounded around the event’s own timestamp, and strategies never receive the index, so attribution cannot leak future information into decisions.

6.6 Fill rules

An order accumulates one candidate fill from every ReachEvent. A candidate records the item timestamp, our limit price (a maker fills at its own price), the historical reduction as volume, the mechanism, and the evidence rank.

MechanismWhenWhy it is ours
LEVELEvent price equals our price, order is behind us, nothing aheadFIFO: once the ahead set is empty we are next to trade.
INTERCEPTEvent price is worse than ours and at the touchOur better price would have received that taker flow first.
CROSS_ARRIVALOpposite-side add crosses our priceCounterparty that rested historically would have traded with us.

For a sweep of size S, the quantity beyond the ahead set is the sum of LEVEL reductions at our price and INTERCEPT reductions at worse prices, so the two do not double count.

Settlement. For policy p with threshold r and order quantity q:

cumulative_p(t) = min( q, sum of volume over candidates with rank >= r and ts < min(t, expiry, end) )
status_p        = FILLED if cumulative = q; PARTIAL if 0 < cumulative < q; UNFILLED otherwise
first_fill_ts   = ts of the first candidate that adds a positive amount

Candidates are logged unclipped and the order stays live until the horizon unless conservative is already complete. This is what makes one pass exact for all policies and all horizons shorter than the simulated maximum.

6.9 Consistency with the historical reconstruction

  • Apply, then trust. Each item is applied to the book first. Only if its checksum passes do its events reach trackers (I7).

  • No shared mutable state. level_orders and BookView return copies; trackers keep their own ahead dicts.

  • Parity test. Replay the same recording with plain L3Book and with SimL3Book plus live orders; compare levels, order maps, synced and every checksum result after every item.

  • Audit mode. After each item, for each live order check I3: ahead IDs exist at our price in the book and their quantities sum to ahead_qty. A failure means a tracker bug or a mismatch with the keep policy for quantity increases.

  • Validated zone. At placement all three strategies are at most about one tick from the touch, so the queue read is normally inside the top 10 levels. The per-order max_level_rank and checksum_covered fields say when that stops being true.

7. Replay Algorithm (Req. D)

7.1 Clock and ordering rules

RuleStatement
R1Processing order is file order: files sorted by name (= time), records in line order, items in the data array order, events in list order (all bids, then all asks, as L3Book does). Nothing is ever sorted by timestamp.
R2The clock is the item timestamp (matching-engine message time), parsed to integer nanoseconds. Event-level timestamps are metadata only: they can be old (insert time) and are not an ordering key.
R3Monotone guard. ts_eff = max(ts, last_ts_eff). A regression is logged as TS_REGRESSION; stream order is unchanged.
R4Strict-before decisions. An action due at T fires before the first item with ts_eff >= T. A strategy therefore sees only items with timestamp below T.
R5Insert before equal timestamps. An order inserted at T can be affected by every item with ts_eff >= T, including items at exactly T.
R6Expiry. An order inserted at T_i with horizon H expires before the first item with ts_eff >= T_i + H.
R7Same timestamp, many items. No ordering is assumed beyond R1. All requests due at the same T share one BookView.
R8Apply, then trust. Events reach trackers only after the item’s checksum passed.
R9Trades are matched, never decided on. Trade lookups use a bounded window around an event’s own timestamp; strategies cannot reach TradeIndex.
R10recv_ns is used only for gap detection. It is the local clock and is not an ordering key.

Why strict-before, in one example. Items in stream order: i1 (ts 10.000), i2 (ts 10.500, adds B9 at our price), i3 (ts 10.500, modifies B1), i4 (ts 11.000). A request at T = 10.500 fires before i2: its view contains i1 only, even though i2 and i3 share the timestamp. The order is inserted before i2, so B9 lands behind us and i3 can affect us. The cost is a small optimism when many orders are added at exactly T; the latency sweep in Phase 4 measures it. A request at T = 10.700 fires before i4, with a view built from i3.

7.3 Pseudocode

def run(files, schedule, strategies, cfg, trades=None):
    state = {s: SymbolState(s) for s in cfg.symbols}
    for req in schedule:                                   # pre-generated from the clock only
        push(state[req.symbol], Action(DECIDE, due=req.ts_ns, request=req))
    books, meta = {}, None

    for rec in RecordingReader(files=files, gap_ns=cfg.gap_ns):   # StreamRecord(file, line_no, seq, recv_ns, kind, payload)
        if rec.kind == 'meta':                             # new file or writer session
            meta = rec.payload
            for s in state.values(): discontinuity(s, 'SESSION_RESTART')
            books.clear()
            continue
        if rec.kind == 'gap':                              # reader marker: recv_ns jumped by more than gap_ns
            for s in state.values(): discontinuity(s, 'RECV_GAP', needs_validation=True)
            continue
        if rec.kind == 'truncated':                        # reader marker: rest of the file unreadable
            for s in state.values(): discontinuity(s, 'TRUNCATED')
            continue
        msg = rec.payload
        if msg.get('channel') != 'level3' or msg.get('type') not in ('snapshot', 'update'):
            continue                                       # heartbeat / status: clock only
        for item in msg['data']:
            if item['symbol'] in state:
                process_item(state[item['symbol']], msg['type'], item, rec)
    finish(state)                                          # end of data
    return build_result(state)

def process_item(s, mtype, item, rec):
    ts = guard_monotone(s, parse_rfc3339_ns(item['timestamp']))       # R3
    if mtype == 'snapshot' and s.has_synced_before:
        discontinuity(s, 'RESNAPSHOT')                     # BEFORE releasing actions: the hole may precede them
    release_due(s, ts)                                     # R4 to R6: fires every action with due <= ts
    book = books.setdefault(s.symbol, SimL3Book(pair_info(meta, s.symbol), meta['depth']))
    ok = book.apply(mtype, item, position=rec)             # the ONLY write to the historical book
    events = book.last_events                              # kept even when ok is not True

    if mtype == 'snapshot':
        s.synced = ok is True; s.has_synced_before |= s.synced; s.last_good_ts = ts
        return
    if ok is None:                                         # update before any snapshot
        s.counters['unsynced'] += 1; return
    if ok is False:                                        # checksum mismatch
        discontinuity(s, 'DESYNC'); s.synced = False; return          # events discarded (R8)
    s.synced = True; s.needs_validation = False            # ok is True: item is trusted
    evidence = classifier.classify_item(s.symbol, events)  # once per event
    for ev in events:
        for order in s.live.values():
            reach = order.tracker.on_event(ev)
            if reach: fills.record(order, reach, evidence.get((ev.position.seq, ev.event_index)))
    s.last_good_ts = ts
    for order in list(s.live.values()):
        if not book.level_visible(order.book_side, order.price): end(s, order, 'OUT_OF_DEPTH', ts)
        elif fills.conservative_complete(order):             end(s, order, 'ALL_POLICIES_FILLED', ts)
    if cfg.audit_invariants: check_invariants(s, book)

def release_due(s, boundary):
    while s.actions and s.actions[0].due <= boundary:
        a = heappop(s.actions)
        if   a.kind == EXPIRE: expire(s, a.order, a.due)
        elif a.kind == DECIDE: decide(s, a)
        elif a.kind == INSERT: insert(s, a)

def decide(s, a):
    req = a.request
    if not s.synced or s.needs_validation:  return skip_all(req, 'UNSYNCED')
    view = books[s.symbol].view(req.ts_ns, tick(req.symbol), cfg.view_depth)   # ONE view for all strategies
    for strat in strategies:
        out = strat.decide(req, view)
        if isinstance(out, Skip): record_skip(req, strat, out.reason); continue
        order = SimulatedOrder.new(req, strat.name, out, qty=cfg.order_qty[req.symbol], decided_ts=req.ts_ns)
        push(s, Action(INSERT, due=req.ts_ns + cfg.latency_ns, order=order))

def insert(s, a):
    o, book = a.order, books[s.symbol]
    if not s.synced or s.needs_validation or o.decided_ts < s.last_discontinuity_ts:
        return o.skip('DATA_GAP')
    opp = book.best_ask() if o.side == BUY else book.best_bid()
    if opp is not None and crosses(o.side, o.price, opp):   return o.skip('CROSSED_AT_INSERT')
    if not book.level_visible(o.book_side, o.price):         return o.skip('OUT_OF_DEPTH')
    o.start(inserted_ts=a.due, ahead=book.level_orders(o.book_side, o.price),
            rank=book.level_rank(o.book_side, o.price), touch_qty=book.touch_qty(o.book_side))
    s.live[o.order_id] = o
    push(s, Action(EXPIRE, due=a.due + cfg.horizon_ns, order=o))

def discontinuity(s, reason, needs_validation=False):
    for o in s.live.values(): o.finish('CENSORED_' + reason, end_ts=s.last_good_ts)
    s.live.clear(); s.synced = False if reason != 'RECV_GAP' else s.synced
    s.needs_validation |= needs_validation; s.last_discontinuity_ts = s.last_ts_eff
    drop_pending_inserts(s); log_quality(s, reason)

def finish(state):
    for s in state.values():
        for o in s.live.values(): o.finish('CENSORED_END_OF_DATA', end_ts=s.last_good_ts)
        for a in s.actions: record_unreached(a)            # requests after the data ended

Two ordering details matter. A RESNAPSHOT discontinuity is raised before due actions are released: every order still alive at that point has an expiry later than the last good item, and events may be missing between that item and the snapshot, so all of them are censored. For a failing checksum the opposite holds: actions due up to the failing item’s timestamp fire normally, because the data up to the previous item was valid.

8.5 Strategies (strategies.py)

class OrderPlacementStrategy(Protocol):
    name: str
    def decide(self, req: PlacementRequest, view: BookView) -> OrderIntent | Skip: ...

class JoinBestStrategy:
    name = 'join_best'
    def decide(self, req, view):
        px = view.best(req.side)                              # best bid for BUY, best ask for SELL
        return Skip(NO_TOUCH) if px is None else OrderIntent(req.side, px)

class ImproveQuoteStrategy:
    name = 'improve_quote'
    def decide(self, req, view):
        bid, ask = view.best_bid, view.best_ask
        if bid is None or ask is None:  return Skip(ONE_SIDED_BOOK)
        px = bid + view.tick if req.side is BUY else ask - view.tick
        if (req.side is BUY and px >= ask) or (req.side is SELL and px <= bid):
            return Skip(SPREAD_TOO_NARROW)                    # would lock or cross: spread < 2 ticks
        return OrderIntent(req.side, px)

class QuoteDeeperStrategy:
    name = 'quote_deeper'
    def decide(self, req, view):
        best = view.best(req.side)
        if best is None:  return Skip(NO_TOUCH)
        px = best - view.tick if req.side is BUY else best + view.tick
        return Skip(INVALID_PRICE) if px <= 0 else OrderIntent(req.side, px)

Strategies are pure functions of (request, view). Size, latency and expiry are not their concern (P1). improve_quote places only when the spread is at least two ticks, which for liquid pairs may be a minority of timestamps; §11.4 explains how comparisons handle that.

9.2 Counterfactual market impact

Historical events were produced by participants who never saw our order. Replaying them as if it had existed assumes our order changes nothing about what they do. That is most defensible for a tiny order that joins an existing queue and least defensible for a quote that changes the touch.

StrategyHow the assumption can failDirection of the likely error
join_bestJoining the touch lengthens the queue by our size; others deeper in the queue may react to queue length.Small for small sizes.
improve_quoteA new best price can attract more taker flow, change routing, or make competing makers re-quote ahead of us or cancel. The design does not try to correct for this. It limits the exposure instead: fixed small size, a shared horizon, an exogenous-flow assumption stated in every report, and sensitivity runs that vary size and latency.

9.3 Limits of replaying events as though our order existed

  • Reactive behaviour. Cancels and re-quotes by other participants may depend on queue size or on who is at the touch. Neither is modeled.

  • Fill removes liquidity. If our order had filled, later historical trades at that level would have found less liquidity. We do not adjust later events.

  • Overlapping hypothetical orders. Orders from different requests are independent experiments. Two orders at the same price may both claim the same historical execution. This is fine for per-order fill probability but not for portfolio-level capacity or PnL.

  • Crossing arrivals. A resting opposite-side order that would have crossed us (CROSS_ARRIVAL) may have been post-only; that is why it only counts in the optimistic policy.

9.4 Ambiguous trade-to-order matching

  • Trade messages have no maker order ID; the only link is price, side, quantity and time.

  • Several trades can share one message and still come from different takers, so a batch cannot be treated as one order.

  • One historical reduction can be explained by several trades, and one trade by several reductions. The FIFO group matching in §6.5.4 resolves quantity but not identity.

  • The two streams have different latencies. A trade can be recorded before or after its L3 event, so matching uses exchange timestamps and a calibrated window.

9.5 Unknown or uncertain queue priority

  • Insertion at exactly the same exchange timestamp as other orders is ambiguous. Strict-before puts us ahead of orders added at that timestamp (slightly optimistic).

  • Real orders take time to arrive. latency_ns = 0 is idealized; fill probability is overstated by an amount the latency sweep measures.

  • Amend rules: a quantity increase sends an order to the back [Kraken docs]. How the L3 feed shows that is unverified (OQ-2). increase_policy brackets both readings.

  • Iceberg orders: the visible slice is in L3, the hidden quantity is not, and a reload goes to the back of the visible queue [Kraken docs]. Real queue ahead of us can therefore be larger than the recorded queue (OQ-9).

9.6 Missed events and partial coverage

  • A checksum failure, reconnect, resnapshot or receive gap means events may be missing. These end orders as censored; they are never read as non-fills.

  • The checksum covers only the top 10 levels, so queue state deeper than that is unvalidated. Orders report checksum_covered.

  • L3 depth is a subscription choice (100 by default in record.py). Our price can leave it (OUT_OF_DEPTH).

  • The feed excludes in-flight orders, unmatched market orders, untriggered stops and hidden iceberg quantity [Kraken docs].

  • Trade attribution assumes the public stream ran during the same interval and had no holes; feed_healthy is the guard.

9.8 Initial fill model

The initial model is the one described in §6: FIFO insertion at the back; queue advancement by any ahead removal; reach test with touch rule; candidate fills labeled by evidence; three bracketing policies; no impact; fixed small size; censoring on any discontinuity. It makes no claim to a point estimate in L3-only mode. With trades (Phase 3) the balanced policy becomes the headline estimate and the other two its bounds.

9.9 Possible improvements, in rough order of value for effort

  1. Phase 3 trade attribution and FIFO group matching (already planned).

  2. Latency distribution instead of a constant, and a latency sweep.

  3. A calibrated probability that an ambiguous touch reduction is an execution, estimated per regime from the ratio of trade volume to touch reductions, replacing hard rank thresholds.

  4. Adaptive strategies that cancel or re-price when the touch moves (needs the engine to accept cancel intents).

  5. Impact sensitivity: for improve_quote, scale intercepted flow by a factor below 1 and report the break-even factor.

  6. Validation against real fills: a small live experiment would calibrate the model, but it needs trading permissions and an explicit team decision; the current key is read-only by design.

10.3 Strategy and replay properties

  • Strategy independence. For the same schedule and recording, running strategies separately and together gives identical per-strategy results (the engine never couples them).

  • Same view. All strategies receive the same BookView object for a request (identity check).

  • No lookahead. A recording-aware test strategy records view.ts_ns and the item positions it can see; assert every item it saw has timestamp below T. A planted “future” item that would change the best bid must not appear in the view.

  • Batch versus incremental. Feed a stream to run, and again through process_record split at every possible record boundary (including inside a snapshot-to-update run); orders.jsonl, fills.jsonl and quality.jsonl must be byte-identical.

  • Historical replay correctness. On a real or generated recording, per-symbol counts of snapshots, updates, checksum_ok, checksum_fail and skipped_unsynced equal the numbers validate_recording.py prints.

  • Parity (I1). After every item, SimL3Book equals plain L3Book: levels with order lists, orders, synced, timestamp, event_counts, and the checksum result.

  • Policy ordering (I6). On random event streams: conservative <= balanced <= optimistic cumulative fill for every order and every horizon.

  • Determinism. Same inputs and seed produce byte-identical outputs.

10.4 Property tests without new dependencies

A seeded generator creates valid L3 streams (adds, cancels, reductions, sweeps, snapshots, occasional corrupted checksums) with a model book computing the expected checksum. Properties: I1 to I7, no negative quantities, every order ends with exactly one end or skip reason, fills never precede insertion or follow end_ts, and the audit-mode I3 never fails on valid streams.

10.5 Real-data smoke checks (Phase 1 onward)

  • Every order has an end or skip reason; none are left LIVE.

  • The fraction of fifo_anomaly per order is near zero; a high rate means the queue model or the keep policy is wrong.

  • Censored share is consistent with the gap and failure counts reported by validate_recording.py.

  • Hand audit: pick 10 random orders and trace each against the raw lines by event_ref; the ahead set, advancement and candidate fills must match by eye.

11.4 Comparing strategies fairly

  1. Paired design. All strategies are evaluated at the same requests (same timestamps and sides, same fixed size and horizon, same BookView). Compare within a request, then aggregate.

  2. Three denominators. Report (a) unconditional per request, counting a skip as no order and no fill; (b) the common-support subset where all three strategies placed; (c) per strategy conditional on placement. improve_quote skips whenever the spread is below two ticks, so (a) and (b) can differ a lot.

  3. Coverage filter by data quality only. Exclude a request if any discontinuity for its symbol falls inside [T, T + H], using quality.jsonl. The filter depends on the data, not on fills, so it cannot select on outcomes. For a shorter horizon H', use H'.

  4. Censoring is never a non-fill. Observed outcomes only; for time-to-fill use Kaplan-Meier with unfilled-at-horizon as censored at H.

  5. Schedule fixed in advance. Generate schedule.jsonl from the clock with a seed before looking at any outcome; never choose times by book state or by what happened next.

  6. Dependence. Overlapping orders are correlated. Use a spacing of at least H for the primary analysis, or a block bootstrap by time block (for example 10 minutes) for confidence intervals.

  7. Bounds, not points. Report conservative, balanced and optimistic side by side. A ranking claim is firm only if it holds under all three.

  8. Sensitivity grid. Horizon (several values from one run), size, latency_ns (for example 0, 50 ms, 250 ms, 1 s), increase_policy.

  • OQ-14 (checksum failures): measured at 376 failures, about 0.005% of updates, but frequent in time (about one per symbol every four minutes on average).

  • OQ-2 and OQ-18 (queue priority on increases): open. Only 26k modify events exist, and the code cannot show whether Kraken re-queues on an increase; keep increase_policy.

  • OQ-4 and OQ-5 (trade timing and completeness): open. The audit confirmed the trade fields and that the two streams are independent, but did not join trades to L3.

  • OQ-20 (new): at an hour boundary, do the old-subscription updates at the start of the next file continue the previous book? Default: drop them (clear books at the meta line, as validate_recording.py does) and censor live orders at the boundary.

Assumptions carried by the design

  • Price-time priority; our order is last at insertion (A1).

  • Taker flow is exogenous to our order (A2).

  • Order size is small relative to the touch (A3).

  • Within validated segments the L3 stream is complete for the visible depth (A4).

  • Timestamps behave as documented, subject to OQ-1, OQ-4 and OQ-11 (A6).

  • Tick size is constant per symbol (A7).

  • Hypothetical orders are independent of each other and of the book (no impact).
